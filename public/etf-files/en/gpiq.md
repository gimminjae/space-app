# GPIQ: How Much Nasdaq Exposure Should Be Covered?

When a Nasdaq income ETF promises a monthly schedule, the next question is how much. Apply GPIQ’s reported distribution measure to several investment sizes. Once the cash scale is clear, examine the choices the fund makes to generate it.

## What could $10,000 pay each month?

On the assumptions below, **$10,000.00 represents $82.17 per month before tax**. This gives the percentage a practical scale: a contribution toward a bill, or a larger part of a spending budget.

The input is the issuer’s **9.86% trailing 12-month distribution yield, dated 2026-09-30** ([official source](https://am.gs.com/en-us/advisors/funds/detail/PV105259/38149W630/goldman-sachs-nasdaq-100-premium-income-etf)). The table applies that percentage directly to each investment amount to illustrate the scale of cash income.

| Investment budget | Gross monthly equivalent | Gross annual equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| $1,000.00 | $8.22 | $98.60 |
| $10,000.00 | $82.17 | $986.00 |
| $50,000.00 | $410.83 | $4,930.00 |
| $100,000.00 | $821.67 | $9,860.00 |

These are **before-tax illustrations**. The full budget is assumed invested, allowing fractional shares. Annual cash is divided by twelve to produce a monthly equivalent; actual monthly payments vary. Investor taxes and trading costs are excluded, and distributions are not reinvested. Fund expenses already reflected in the reported cash measure are not deducted a second time.

### Working backward from $500 or $1,000 a month

| Gross monthly cash target | Capital at the same assumptions |
|---|---:|
| $500.00 | about $60,900 |
| $1,000.00 | about $121,800 |

The formula is monthly target × 12 ÷ annual distribution rate. Required capital is rounded up to the next $100. A 20% payment cut turns a $500.00 monthly target into $400.00 at the same holding size. Use these numbers to size a cash-flow plan, not as a promise of future payments.

Income inputs additionally reviewed: 2026-10-23.

## Choosing assets and choosing coverage

Changing the equity foundation changes the outcome even when an option strategy sounds similar. Compared with GPIX's broad US large-cap starting point, GPIQ is more closely associated with the Nasdaq-100's composition and concentration. Splitting an investment between the two does not automatically split its economic risks into independent halves. Large companies can overlap.

| Item | How to read GPIQ |
|---|---|
| Launch | October 24, 2023 |
| Equity exposure | Large US growth-oriented companies around the Nasdaq-100 |
| Income approach | An adjustable option-writing overlay |
| Payments | Monthly, without a guaranteed amount |
| Gross annual expenses | 0.35% |
| Net annual expenses | 0.29%, reflecting a waiver through at least April 30, 2027 |

The issuer's fee waiver has contractual conditions. Recording both figures makes a later change easier to interpret. Lower expenses help, but a few basis points cannot erase differences in stocks or derivative structures. One basis point equals 0.01 percentage point. The fund's actual return still depends far more on what its investments do than on this small arithmetic exercise.

## What comes with a larger premium?

An option's price reflects several inputs, including its underlying asset, strike, time to expiration and expected volatility. The possibility of larger price moves can increase what buyers are willing to pay. That is not simply better news for the seller: the underlying stocks can also suffer substantial losses.

Adjusting option coverage does not secure an identical monthly distribution. Collecting more premium and preserving more room for appreciation involve a trade-off. Managers can change coverage as conditions evolve, but the market path afterward can differ from the one they anticipated.

![The exchange between premium income and upside in a simple covered call](/etf-assets/covered-call.en.svg)

This is a conceptual illustration, not GPIQ performance. A real fund rolls and adjusts multiple contracts. A sharp decline followed by a rapid rebound can be particularly revealing: the portfolio may participate in the fall while an overlay limits some of the recovery. Receiving cash each month does not establish that the journey was comfortable.

## Before translating a rate into a household budget

Multiplying a recent payment by twelve and dividing by a share price produces an annualized figure. It assumes repetition. It is different from adding the payments actually received over the previous year, and both measures differ from total return, which also considers the value remaining invested.

Consider what would happen if distributions fell. Then consider whether a simultaneous decline in the fund would force sales to cover spending. Those two developments can reinforce each other in a household budget. Monthly payments do not transfer responsibility for spending reserves from the investor to the fund.

For a Korean investor, also separate the dollar result from the won result. Exchange-rate changes and conversion charges affect money eventually spent in won. Rising dollar distributions do not guarantee rising won receipts. Equally, an exchange-rate gain should not be attributed entirely to the manager's stock selection or option decisions.

An investor who reinvests every payment faces another useful question: is the distribution schedule itself helping meet the goal? Reinvestment can support compounding, but the cash was not created outside the fund's economics. An ex-distribution adjustment and personal tax treatment can matter even when the deposit is immediately invested again.

## Put the mechanism above the ranking

When comparing GPIQ, JEPQ and QQQI, begin with the stocks and contracts behind the cash. Direct index options and equity-linked notes are not interchangeable structures. Then align the comparison dates and examine reinvested total return, drawdowns and expenses together.

GPIQ's live record begins in 2023. A precise-looking table showing wealth and distributions ten years from now is a calculation built on assumptions, not an observed outcome. Distinguishing those two categories lets an investor appreciate regular income while still acknowledging the risks of the growth stocks underneath it.
