# RISE 200 Weekly Covered Call: Why Sell Options Each Week?

## What could ₩10 million pay each month?

On the assumptions below, **₩10,000,000 represents ₩227,040 per month before tax**. This gives the percentage a practical scale: a contribution toward a bill, or a larger part of a spending budget.

Use the **2026-10-21 closing market price of ₩10,560** and the **₩240 per share paid on 2026-10-02**, with a record date of 2026-09-30 ([official price and payment history](https://kbam.co.kr/products/44G3)). A ₩10 million budget buys 946 whole shares in this illustration: 946 × ₩240 = ₩227,040. The remaining ₩10,240 stays uninvested.

| Investment budget | Gross monthly equivalent | Gross annual equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| ₩10,000,000 | ₩227,040 | ₩2,724,480 |
| ₩30,000,000 | ₩681,600 | ₩8,179,200 |
| ₩50,000,000 | ₩1,136,160 | ₩13,633,920 |
| ₩100,000,000 | ₩2,272,560 | ₩27,270,720 |

These are **before-tax illustrations**. Shares are rounded down to whole units. The recent payment is assumed to repeat monthly, and the annual figure is twelve times that amount. Buying now does not create an entitlement to a payment whose record date has passed. Investor taxes and trading costs are excluded, and distributions are not reinvested. Fund expenses already reflected in the reported cash measure are not deducted a second time.

### Working backward from ₩500,000 or ₩1 million a month

| Gross monthly cash target | Capital at the same assumptions |
|---|---:|
| ₩500,000 | about ₩22,010,000 |
| ₩1,000,000 | about ₩44,010,000 |

Divide the income target by the payment per share, round the share count up, and multiply by the quoted price. Required capital is rounded up to the next ₩10,000. A 20% payment cut turns a ₩500,000 monthly target into ₩400,000 at the same holding size. Use these numbers to size a cash-flow plan, not as a promise of future payments.

This RISE example produces an especially large figure because it applies September’s ₩240 payment to a lower October share price. Those are two different observation dates, and repetition is uncertain. Large cash payments can coexist with a falling share price; follow subsequent payments and changes in net asset value together.

## Selling rights near the current price

The underlying KOSPI 200 Weekly Covered Call ATM index uses at-the-money options. ATM means a strike near the underlying's current level when the option is selected. The buyer pays for contractual exposure to a favorable move; the seller receives premium and accepts the corresponding obligation.

The issuer's 2026 material describes using weekly options with expirations twice a week. Frequent trading does not mean interest repeatedly appears without a trade-off. Each new contract exchanges upside under a fresh strike and a fresh set of market conditions.

## What a month-end comparison misses

Consider one simplified contract with an underlying price of 100, a strike of 100 and a premium of 2. If the underlying ends at 110, the covered portion's approximate gain is still 2. If it ends at 80, adding the premium leaves a loss around 18. This is a teaching example excluding taxes, costs and dividends, not the ETF's reported performance.

![A separate hypothetical covered-call example uses a strike of 105](/etf-assets/covered-call.en.svg)

The diagram uses a strike of 105, leaving some initial room for appreciation. Distinguish it from the at-the-money example in the paragraph above. Comparing the two makes the strike's role visible: a strike nearer the current price begins exchanging away gains sooner, other conditions held equal.

Rolling contracts repeatedly adds another consideration. Two months with identical starting and ending index levels can produce different strategy returns if the paths between them differ. A decline followed by a new call sale and a fast rebound can leave the portfolio exposed to the fall while limiting part of the recovery.

## A national index still has weights

KOSPI 200 exposure offers access to many prominent Korean companies, but not in equal amounts. Large companies and heavily represented industries can exert substantial influence. Counting constituents alone can conceal concentration rather than explain it.

Someone already holding substantial Korean equities or semiconductor shares should inspect the overlap. Is this fund adding a different economic exposure, or placing an option overlay on familiar risks? Avoiding a currency-conversion step is convenient, but convenience is a separate question from diversification.

Market orders also deserve attention. The displayed fund value, the current exchange price and the bid-ask spread are different observations. A distribution strategy does not remove the need to examine the price at which an order will execute. Paying too much on entry cannot be repaired simply by waiting for the next scheduled payment.

## A monthly target is not an annual promise

A distribution target in promotional material is not a guaranteed interest rate. Payments can change with the market, investment results and distribution policy. Adding monthly payments or compounding an assumed rate into future wealth requires assumptions about both payments and capital value.

Net asset value also adjusts for distributions, all else equal. Cash received and assets remaining belong in the same return calculation. A large payout can coexist with a decline in capital. Likewise, a published reinvested return will not describe the balance of an account whose owner spent every payment.

Using domestic stocks and options does not make every component of every investor's return tax-free. The taxable portion of distributions, account type and individual circumstances require separate attention. Trading expenses and execution spreads can also matter beyond the stated annual fee.

The essential question is not how often premium is collected, but what is exchanged for it each time. A reason for wanting won-denominated cash flow must be considered alongside the possibility of lagging a rally. Understanding that exchange is more valuable than becoming familiar with the payment calendar alone.
