# QDVO: The Trade-Off Between Growth and Monthly Income

Holding growth stocks and receiving monthly cash are both appealing. QDVO brings those aims into one portfolio. Working backward from a $500 monthly target turns a large-looking distribution rate into a concrete capital requirement.

## What could $10,000 pay each month?

On the assumptions below, **$10,000.00 represents $93.25 per month before tax**. This gives the percentage a practical scale: a contribution toward a bill, or a larger part of a spending budget.

The input is the issuer’s **11.19% annualized recent distribution rate, dated 2026-09-30** ([official source](https://amplifyetfs.com/qdvo/)). The table applies that percentage directly to each investment amount to illustrate the scale of cash income.

| Investment budget | Gross monthly equivalent | Gross annual equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| $1,000.00 | $9.33 | $111.90 |
| $10,000.00 | $93.25 | $1,119.00 |
| $50,000.00 | $466.25 | $5,595.00 |
| $100,000.00 | $932.50 | $11,190.00 |

These are **before-tax illustrations**. The full budget is assumed invested, allowing fractional shares. Annual cash is divided by twelve to produce a monthly equivalent; actual monthly payments vary. Investor taxes and trading costs are excluded, and distributions are not reinvested. Fund expenses already reflected in the reported cash measure are not deducted a second time.

### Working backward from $500 or $1,000 a month

| Gross monthly cash target | Capital at the same assumptions |
|---|---:|
| $500.00 | about $53,700 |
| $1,000.00 | about $107,300 |

The formula is monthly target × 12 ÷ annual distribution rate. Required capital is rounded up to the next $100. A 20% payment cut turns a $500.00 monthly target into $400.00 at the same holding size. Use these numbers to size a cash-flow plan, not as a promise of future payments.

Income inputs additionally reviewed: 2026-10-23.

## Start with the objective

The Amplify CWP Growth & Income ETF describes capital appreciation as its primary objective and high current income as secondary. It selects large-cap growth equities and can use tactical covered calls on individual stocks and, at times, broad market indexes. It should not be treated as a simple Nasdaq-100 tracker.

The reviewed issuer page lists total annual expenses of 0.56% and monthly distributions. That updates the earlier 0.55% figure. The earlier description of a permanently fixed option limit should also not be treated as a contractual feature without checking the current prospectus and actual positions.

| Item | Official observation |
|---|---|
| Total annual expenses | 0.56% |
| Launch | August 2024 |
| Payment schedule | Monthly |
| Annualized distribution rate | 11.19%, September 30, 2026 |
| 30-day SEC yield | 0.39%, same date |
| Strategy | Selected growth stocks with tactical call options |

The difference between the two yield measures is not automatically an error. Option-related cash and the classification of distributions interact differently with different measures. A distribution rate should not be translated into a statement that the fund earned that percentage as an investment return.

## Options sit on top of equity risk

Growth-stock prices often reflect substantial expectations about future earnings. Changes in rates or business expectations can produce large price movements. Receiving call premiums does not eliminate that underlying risk. A large cash payment and a small capital loss are separate questions.

![Hypothetical covered-call diagram: premium income does not remove a large decline in the underlying asset](/etf-assets/covered-call.en.svg)

Uncovered holdings retain their own upside, while written calls can reduce gains on the exposure they cover. The ability to adjust an overlay gives the manager flexibility. It does not prove that each adjustment will turn out well. Whether a particular decision helped becomes clearer only after the market has moved.

## Count the weights, not just the names

An ETF can hold dozens of securities and still depend heavily on its largest positions. It can also overlap with other growth funds in the same account. Owning two tickers in equal proportions does not mean the economic risks have been divided into two independent halves.

Add the weights of the largest five equity holdings, then compare those names with any Nasdaq or technology funds you already own. The exercise turns a general concern about concentration into a concrete portfolio question. “Growth plus income” does not mean the same thing as “stocks plus cash.”

This also explains why a fund's number of reported positions needs context. Cash and option positions can appear beside stocks in a holdings file. A count alone may tell less about equity diversification than the actual weights and the exposures represented by derivatives.

## A short record leaves unanswered questions

Launched in August 2024, QDVO had not experienced a long sequence of complete market cycles by this October 2026 review. There is no actual five- or ten-year ETF performance history to report. Filling those empty columns with another index or an invented annual return would blur the evidence.

Multiplying the latest distribution by twelve to set next year's spending budget creates a similar problem. Market conditions and distribution decisions can change. Someone spending the cash should first test a reduced-payment scenario. Someone reinvesting should compare total returns over matching dates.

Published NAV returns generally already reflect the fund's expenses. Subtracting those expenses again can double count them. Personal taxes and currency conversion costs, however, are not automatically included. Read the definition of the performance measure before doing more arithmetic.

QDVO is a particular way to hold growth exposure while receiving cash along the way. Its equity risk, option opportunity cost and limited operating history belong in the same decision. Once those are visible, the investment can be described more accurately than simply “getting growth and dividends at once.”
