# ACE Global Income TOP10: An ETF That Owns Ten ETFs

If choosing ten income ETFs feels difficult, could you buy one ETF that chooses the ten for you? ACE Global Income TOP10 makes sense from that starting point. But adding another wrapper does not remove the risks inside it. It adds another layer to inspect.

“Global” and “income” are useful labels only after we ask separate questions: where are the underlying funds listed, what do they own, and where does their cash come from? Those answers need not be identical.

## Five equity funds and five bond funds

ACE Global Income TOP10, Korean ticker 460960, is managed by Korea Investment Management. Its benchmark is the Solactive Global Superior Income TOP 10 Price Return Index. The issuer describes ten US-listed ETFs selected with attention to distribution yield and payment consistency: five equity ETFs and five bond ETFs.

Five plus five describes the number of funds. It should not be read as a promise that the portfolio's market value is always exactly half equities and half bonds. Index rules, price changes and rebalancing affect the actual weights. Read the weight column, not just the list of names.

The fee was checked against exchange product information alongside the issuer material. NAV and exchange price are different concepts, even when both appear on the same screen. Neither a historical NAV nor a historical market price is a quotation for your next order.

## Opening the boxes inside the box

An equity ETF may collect corporate dividends, but it may also write options. In the latter case, the outer fund indirectly inherits the trade-off between cash premiums and some potential upside. A bond ETF can own long-duration government debt, corporate credit or lower-rated obligations. These exposures do not behave alike.

| Visible diversification | The next question |
|---|---|
| Ten ETF tickers | How much do their underlying exposures overlap? |
| Bond funds included | What are their duration and credit quality? |
| US exchange listings | Which countries and currencies drive the assets? |
| Monthly payments | What portion comes from dividends, interest or other sources? |

Two equity funds can hold many of the same large companies. Long-duration bonds can fall when yields rise. Riskier credit can decline alongside equities during economic stress. The word “bond” does not guarantee a reliable offset to every stock-market loss.

## Expenses exist at more than one level

For a fund of funds, the outer fund's disclosed fee is only the starting point. Underlying ETF expenses are reflected in those funds' values and therefore affect the outer fund's results. Remembering 0.24% as an all-inclusive cost would miss that structure.

Consider an invented basket with half its assets in a fund costing 0.4% and half in one costing 0.6%. The simple weighted underlying expense is 0.5%, before the outer layer's own expenses. This is an explanation of the mechanism, not a calculation of ACE's actual total cost. That requires current weights and the applicable expense disclosures.

The same care applies to performance. If a published return already reflects fund expenses, subtracting all those expenses again would double count them. Understand whether you are constructing a forecast from assumptions or reading an actual net performance series.

## Trading in won does not settle the currency question

A Korean listing makes buying and receiving cash in won convenient. Foreign assets still reach the fund's won value through currency translation. Hedging policy and actual hedging exposure should be checked in the current prospectus; the currency used to place an order is not proof of a hedge.

In a hypothetical unchanged foreign portfolio, a 10% fall in the dollar's won value would hurt the unhedged dollar portion in won terms. The opposite move could help. Changes in local-currency distributions therefore need not come entirely from changes in the underlying investment strategy.

The convenience of this ETF is the ability to hold several income exposures through one position. Understanding that convenience requires looking one level deeper at assets and costs. Outsourcing selection can reduce the work of placing trades; it does not remove the need to know what the investment contains.
